# Go-to-Market & Positioning Framework

## 1. Framework Overview

The Go-to-Market (GTM) and Positioning Framework guides product teams through crystallizing how to launch and scale a product in its target market. This framework helps product managers articulate who the customer is, why they need the product, how it compares to alternatives, and the tactical steps to acquire the first customers and scale.

### When to Use This Framework

- **Pre-launch planning**: Before building or after achieving product-market fit validation
- **New market entry**: Entering an adjacent market or vertical with an existing product
- **Positioning refinement**: Clarifying messaging across sales, marketing, and investor conversations
- **Launch phase gates**: Using metrics to decide when to move from beta to controlled launch to scale
- **Competitive response**: Reframing positioning when new competitors emerge
- **Fundraising preparation**: Grounding investor conversations in realistic customer acquisition and unit economics

## 2. Go-to-Market & Positioning Template

### Target Customer

**Definition**: The specific buyer and end-user personas, their industry, company size, and key pain points they experience.

**Key elements to define**:
- Primary buyer title and role
- Company size range (employees, revenue)
- Industry/vertical and geography
- Typical annual budget for this category
- Top 3 pain points that motivate purchase
- Urgency and purchase timeline

### Value Proposition

**Definition**: The specific, quantifiable outcome the product delivers relative to the status quo (manual work, legacy tools, or doing nothing).

**Key elements to define**:
- Top 1-2 metrics that improve (time saved, quality uplift, cost reduction)
- Quantifiable impact (e.g., "50% reduction in drafting time")
- Context for how they use the product today
- Why the product solves this pain, not alternatives

### Positioning Statement

**Definition**: A 1-2 sentence statement that defines how the product is perceived relative to other solutions.

**Key elements to define**:
- Product category ("the X for Y")
- Key differentiator vs. competing approaches
- Tone (e.g., "professional-grade," "accessible," "enterprise-ready")
- What the product is NOT

### Competitive Landscape

**Definition**: The set of alternatives customers consider, ranked by strength and specific weaknesses relative to the product.

**Key elements to define**:
- Direct competitors and their key strengths
- Indirect competitors (broader category solutions)
- Build-vs.-buy and status quo (manual work) as competitors
- Win-loss analysis: Why customers pick your product vs. others
- Sustainable competitive advantages (moat)

### Go-to-Market Strategy

**Definition**: The phased plan for launching and scaling the product, with clear success metrics and go/no-go gates.

**Key elements to define**:
- Phase 1: Beta launch (user count, timelines, feedback focus)
- Phase 2: Controlled launch (pricing, marketing channels, target volume)
- Phase 3: Scaling (partnership models, expansion motions, new segments)
- Success metrics and phase gates (e.g., NPS, CAC, usage metrics)

### Customer Acquisition & Economics

**Definition**: The unit economics that demonstrate the business model is sustainable and profitable at scale.

**Key elements to define**:
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) by channel
- Lifetime Value (LTV) and payback period
- LTV:CAC ratio (target: 3:1 or higher)
- Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) and expansion revenue
- Churn rate and retention curve
- Path to profitability

### Messaging & Communication

**Definition**: Different messaging framings tailored to distinct audiences (buyers, users, investors, partners).

**Key elements to define**:
- Key message for each audience (buyer, user, investor, partner)
- Supporting proof points and social proof
- Call-to-action and next step
- Tone and voice guidelines

## 3. Realistic Example: AI Writing Assistant

### Target Customer

The primary target is **Content Marketing Managers** at mid-market B2B SaaS companies.

**Profile**:
- Role: Head of Content, Content Lead, or Marketing Manager responsible for blog strategy and production
- Company size: 50-500 employees
- Industry: B2B SaaS and technology companies in high-growth mode
- Annual marketing budget: $500K–$5M
- Geography: North America and Western Europe (English-speaking)

**Top pain points**:
1. **Time constraint**: Creating 4-8 blog posts per month is a bottleneck; researching, drafting, and editing consumes 15-20 hours/week
2. **Quality consistency**: Junior writers produce draft content that requires extensive editing to match brand voice and style guidelines
3. **Cost pressure**: Hiring freelance writers ($50-150 per article) adds $2,000-5,000/month to budget; full-time hires are expensive and underutilized in off-months

**Urgency**: High. Marketing teams are measured on lead generation and brand authority. Content bottleneck directly impacts quarterly pipeline targets.

### Value Proposition

The AI Writing Assistant reduces **content drafting time by 50%** while maintaining brand voice consistency.

**Quantified benefits**:
- Time saving: From 3 hours per article to 1.5 hours (focused on editing and strategy, not blank-page writing)
- Productivity: Team can produce 8-12 articles/month instead of 4-6
- Cost reduction: $2,000-3,000/month savings by reducing freelancer dependency
- Quality: Brand voice checklist ensures consistency across writers

**How it works**: Writers input key talking points, target audience, and blog outline. The assistant generates a first draft in brand voice, which the writer edits in 15-20 minutes instead of drafting from scratch.

**Why this matters**: Content marketing is a long-term play (12-18 months to ROI), but teams need output now. The assistant removes the drafting friction without replacing human judgment on strategy or editorial voice.

### Positioning Statement

**"The professional writer's assistant, not a replacement."**

The product is positioned as a **draft accelerator** for in-house content teams, not a fully automated content platform. This distinction is critical: it appeals to teams who care about brand voice and editorial quality (B2B SaaS), not teams seeking cost elimination at the expense of quality.

**What we are**:
- A time-saving draft tool that maintains brand voice
- Built for in-house marketing teams with editorial standards

**What we are NOT**:
- A replacement for human writers
- A low-cost alternative to freelancers (we're complementary)
- A general-purpose AI chatbot (narrow focus on marketing content)

### Competitive Landscape

**Direct competitors**:

1. **ChatGPT** ($20/month)
   - Strength: Brand recognition, low cost, general-purpose
   - Weakness: No brand voice training, requires heavy prompt engineering, marketing-naive outputs (too generic)
   - Why we win: Custom brand voice, marketing-specific best practices, faster turnaround

2. **Copy.ai** ($29/month)
   - Strength: Marketing-focused, affordable
   - Weakness: Output quality is inconsistent, no true draft-length content generation, templates feel templated
   - Why we win: Higher quality, longer-form capability, better editing experience

3. **Jasper** ($125/month)
   - Strength: High-quality outputs, strong brand voice training
   - Weakness: Expensive, complex interface, overkill for small marketing teams, requires AI literacy
   - Why we win: Simple, affordable, faster to value, better UX

**Indirect competitors**:
- Freelance writers on Upwork ($5-50/hour)
- Content agencies ($3,000-8,000/month)
- Hiring another junior writer (+$50K salary + overhead)

**Sustainable advantage**:
- Brand voice model that improves with feedback (data moat)
- Marketing-specific templates and best practices
- Strong user experience (fast, intuitive editing)
- Community and integrations (WordPress, Contentful, Slack)

### Go-to-Market Strategy

**Phase 1: Beta Launch (Months 1-3)**

- **Target**: 20-30 content marketing teams from early adopter networks
- **Channels**: Direct outreach to product/marketing leaders via LinkedIn, relevant Slack communities, content marketing communities (Notion-based cohorts, HubSpot user groups)
- **Pricing**: Free during beta with optional $50 "founder supporter" license
- **Focus**: Feedback on brand voice accuracy, editorial workflow, time savings validation
- **Success metrics**: NPS ≥ 50, ≥ 10 articles published per user/month, <10% weekly churn
- **Go/no-go gate**: Achieve 80% of signups report "significant time savings" (>30% reduction) in exit survey

**Phase 2: Controlled Launch (Months 4-8)**

- **Pricing tiers**:
  - Starter: $29/month (up to 4 drafts/month, 1 brand voice profile)
  - Professional: $79/month (unlimited drafts, 5 brand voices, priority support)
  - Team: $199/month (5 seats, advanced analytics, API access)
- **Target customer acquisition**: 200-500 paying customers
- **Channels**:
  - Content marketing communities and podcasts (sponsorships of 2-3 major shows: The Hubcast, Content Marketing Show)
  - SEO and organic (blog targeting "how to write blog posts faster," "AI for content marketing")
  - Partner integrations (WordPress plugin, Zapier integration)
  - Product hunt launch and secondary launches
- **Launch activities**: Case study with 2-3 design partner teams, webinar series ("Content Velocity in 2026")
- **Success metrics**: Maintain NPS ≥ 50, CAC ≤ $50, MRR growth of 15%/month, 5% monthly churn
- **Go/no-go gate**: Achieve $50K MRR with LTV:CAC ≥ 12:1

**Phase 3: Scale (Months 9+)**

- **Expansion motions**:
  1. **Vertical expansion**: Extend to agencies (reseller model, 20% discount for agencies), corporate communications, internal documentation teams
  2. **Product expansion**: Add AI-powered editing assistant, tone/style detection, performance analytics
  3. **Partnerships**: Native integrations with CMS platforms (WordPress, Webflow), marketing platforms (HubSpot, Marketo), agency software (Monday, Asana)
  4. **Land-and-expand**: Grow ARPU from $50 to $65/month through add-on features and seat expansion
- **Target**: 2,000+ paying customers, $100K+ MRR
- **Success metrics**: Maintain NPS ≥ 55, grow CAC through partnerships (lower cost), achieve 10% monthly growth, push 5-year LTV to $1,500+ per customer
- **Long-term**: Build moat through integrations and brand voice data; position for acquisition by larger marketing platforms or standalone IPO trajectory

### Customer Acquisition & Economics

**Phase 2 Unit Economics (Controlled Launch)**:

- **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)**: $50
  - Assumption: $500/month total marketing spend, acquiring 10 new customers/month
  - Mix: 40% content/SEO (zero cost, 4-month lag), 30% communities ($100/month), 20% partnerships, 10% direct/referral

- **ARPU** (Year 1): $50/customer/month
  - Mix: 60% Starter ($29), 30% Professional ($79), 10% Team ($199)
  - Blended: ~60% * $29 + 30% * $79 + 10% * $199 = $50.60

- **Monthly Churn**: 5% (excellent for SaaS)
- **Lifetime Value (LTV)**: $1,200
  - Calculation: (ARPU × 12 months × Gross Margin) / Churn Rate
  - ($50 × 12 × 90%) / (5% monthly) = ~$1,200
  - Payback period: 50 / 50 = 1.0 month (excellent)

- **LTV:CAC Ratio**: 1:24 (highly profitable; target is 3:1+)

- **Year 2 expansion**: As customers expand from 1-2 seats to 3-5 seats on Team tier, ARPU grows to $65/month
  - Updated LTV: ($65 × 12 × 90%) / 5% = $1,560
  - LTV:CAC improves to 1:31

**Profitability**:
- Gross margin: ~90% (SaaS software, minimal COGS)
- CAC payback: 1 month (highly efficient)
- Break-even: Achieved at ~1,000 customers ($50K MRR) with current burn rate
- Path to profitability: Months 10-12 (Phase 2 → Phase 3 transition)

### Messaging & Communication

**For Content Marketing Managers (Buyers)**:
> "Write 2x more content without hiring more writers. AI Writing Assistant gives your team back 5 hours per week with drafts that sound like your brand, not ChatGPT. Get started free."
- Proof point: "Helps 500+ marketing teams save 50% on content drafting"
- CTA: Free 14-day trial

**For Marketing Executives (Buying Committee)**:
> "Scale content output 40% in 90 days. Our customers cut content costs by $2,000-3,000/month and publish consistently. ROI typically breaks even in 4 weeks."
- Proof point: "Typical payback: 1 month. LTV $1,200 per user."
- CTA: Schedule 15-min discovery call

**For Investors (Seed/Series A)**:
> "We are building the content velocity layer for B2B SaaS marketing. $50B TAM, 40%+ annual market growth, sticky product (90%+ retention), expanding LTV through platform. Early traction shows 24:1 LTV:CAC ratio with clear path to $100M ARR."
- Proof point: "500 beta users, 60%+ month-on-month growth, NPS 58, CAC $50"
- CTA: Investor deck, quarterly metrics

**For Partners (Integrations)**:
> "Add AI-powered content generation to your platform. White-label or native integration. Revenue share model: 30% of net new ARR."
- Proof point: "Average account expansion: 40% of new customer value from integration."
- CTA: Partner agreement, technical specs

## 4. Tips for Positioning & GTM Success

1. **Test assumptions, don't assume product-market fit**: In Phase 1, validate that (a) customers actually save time (measure actual writing hours), (b) output quality matches brand voice expectations (not just stated interest), and (c) they will pay. Use quantitative surveys and interviews to ground positioning in reality, not hope.

2. **Messaging must shift as you scale**: Early adopter messaging ("For teams who care about brand voice") differs from mainstream messaging ("Save time on content, focus on strategy"). Avoid locking into one positioning; instead, map personas to messaging and evolve as the customer base broadens.

3. **Economics discipline prevents wasted spend**: Calculate CAC and LTV upfront and track them monthly. If CAC creeps above $75 while LTV stays flat, the math breaks; fix acquisition channels or improve retention before scaling. Poor unit economics at small scale become catastrophic at large scale.

4. **Competitive positioning is relative, not absolute**: You don't need to be better than Jasper at everything; you need to be meaningfully better at the one thing your target buyer prioritizes (e.g., ease of use + affordability). Clearly articulate the trade-off so customers self-select into the right tool for their needs.

5. **Sales process reflects positioning**: If positioning is "professional assistant for in-house teams," your sales cycle should be 1-2 weeks (demo + trial). If it's "enterprise platform," 8-12 weeks (procurement, IT). Misalignment here kills GTM execution.
