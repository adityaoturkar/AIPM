# Stakeholder Comms Prompts

Prompts for status updates, alignment, buy-in, and escalation.

---

### 1. Executive Status Update Writer

**Use when:** You need to write a crisp, executive-ready update on a product initiative — for a weekly email, Slack post, or meeting pre-read.
**Output type:** Structured status update in BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front) format

---

Write an executive status update for [INITIATIVE NAME].

Here is my raw context:
- What we shipped or completed this week: [NOTES]
- What is currently in progress: [NOTES]
- Blockers or risks: [NOTES]
- Key decisions needed from leadership: [NOTES OR "NONE"]
- Metrics / results so far: [DATA POINTS OR "TOO EARLY"]

Audience: [EXEC TEAM / BOARD / CROSS-FUNCTIONAL STAKEHOLDERS]
Desired length: [1 SHORT PARAGRAPH / STRUCTURED BULLETS / FULL MEMO]
Tone: [CONFIDENT AND CONCISE / TRANSPARENT / CAUTIOUSLY OPTIMISTIC]

Format the update as:
- **BLUF:** One sentence on where things stand
- **Progress:** What got done
- **Next:** What's coming in the next [1–2 WEEKS]
- **Risks/Blockers:** What could derail us and what we need
- **Ask:** Any decisions or support needed (skip if none)

### 2. Difficult Tradeoff Communication

**Use when:** You need to communicate a difficult scope, timeline, or resource tradeoff to stakeholders who will not be happy.
**Output type:** Structured stakeholder message with context, options, and a clear recommendation

---

I need to communicate the following tradeoff to [STAKEHOLDER GROUP]:

Situation: [DESCRIBE WHAT CHANGED OR WHAT YOU DISCOVERED]
What we originally committed to: [ORIGINAL PLAN]
What we can now deliver: [REVISED PLAN]
Why: [ROOT CAUSE — BE HONEST]
Business impact of the change: [DESCRIBE IMPACT]

Options I'm considering presenting:
- Option A: [DESCRIPTION, TRADEOFFS]
- Option B: [DESCRIPTION, TRADEOFFS]
- Option C (if any): [DESCRIPTION, TRADEOFFS]

My recommendation: [OPTION X] because [REASONING]

Write a message that:
1. Leads with the key change and its impact — no burying the lede
2. Explains the root cause honestly without over-explaining or being defensive
3. Presents the options clearly with tradeoffs
4. States my recommendation and the rationale
5. Ends with a clear ask: approval, a decision date, or a meeting request
6. Maintains trust and credibility — acknowledge the inconvenience without being apologetic to the point of undermining confidence

### 3. Cross-Functional Alignment Memo

**Use when:** You are kicking off a new initiative and need to get multiple teams aligned on scope, ownership, and success criteria before work begins.
**Output type:** Alignment memo / one-pager suitable for async review

---

I'm kicking off [INITIATIVE NAME] and need to align the following teams: [LIST TEAMS, e.g., Engineering, Design, Marketing, Sales, Legal].

Context:
- Problem we're solving: [DESCRIPTION]
- Target users: [USER SEGMENT]
- Success metrics: [HOW WE WILL MEASURE SUCCESS]
- Key milestones: [ROUGH TIMELINE WITH DATES]
- Known dependencies: [WHAT THIS WORK DEPENDS ON FROM OTHER TEAMS]
- Known risks: [TOP 2–3 RISKS]
- Out of scope: [WHAT WE ARE NOT DOING]

RACI for key decisions:
- [DECISION 1]: Responsible = [TEAM], Accountable = [PM/LEAD], Consulted = [TEAMS], Informed = [TEAMS]
- [DECISION 2]: (fill in as above)

Write a 1-page alignment memo that:
1. Opens with a concise framing of the opportunity and why now
2. Summarizes scope, timeline, and success criteria
3. Calls out the RACI clearly
4. Lists the 3 questions that must be resolved in the kickoff meeting
5. Specifies what each team needs to do before the kickoff call
